### What Was Challenging

Elena (Junior Dev): "6.3 was materially heavier than I expected going in — it wasn't just 'reuse 8.1's storage,' it needed an additive `pdf_status` migration, a gated renderer dependency, AND it was the first story writing real object bytes to storage (8.1 was metadata-first; 8.2 never landed). Dana (QA Engineer): "And that scope-note gap wasn't just an estimation miss — it directly affected the AC3 lifecycle event requirement. Charlie (Senior Dev): "On the PDF side specifically, `pdf-parse@1.1.1`'s bundled `pdf.js` couldn't parse modern `pdf-lib` output at all — the team had to switch the text extractor to the `pdfjs-dist` legacy build mid-story. That's a real trap for anyone doing PDF-text golden work in the future; it's not documented anywhere outside the retro-notes."

Elena (Junior Dev): "The most serious finding for me, though, was in 6.4's own code review: the sent-lock trigger's 'fail-closed' claim wasn't fully honored on first pass. `status` itself was wholesale-exempt, which meant a `sent→draft` reversal could disarm both locks, and `quote_id`/`id`/`tenant_id`/`created_at` sat in neither the exempt nor the locked tuple — an own-tenant re-parent of a sent commitment was possible. The integration suite at that point only tested content-column mutation, not state-machine reversal or identity/FK columns. That's a real lesson: an immutability trigger test suite needs to specifically probe reversal and identity columns, not just assume 'we tested some mutation, so we tested immutability.'"

Dana (QA Engineer): "On my side: one more instance of the golden-fixture-with-empty-negative-assertion trap. The rot-with-tillval-hidden-and-attachments golden shipped a hidden PDF row with an EMPTY `mustNotAppear` array — so the fixture provided zero negative-assertion coverage, and the hidden-row PDF leak (customer-visible internal content) passed every green gate until the code review caught it directly. A golden with `mustNotAppear: []` doesn't guard anything; it just looks like it does."

Alice (Product Owner): "From a product-decision angle, we're still carrying the same class of item Epic 5 flagged — the correction/PDF affordances rendering on every non-draft version, including terminal states like `superseded`/`rejected`/`expired`. ### Key Insights And Patterns

Bob (Scrum Master): "Here's what stood out across all five stories and the epic-boundary review."

1. **The `pnpm audit` hard-mechanism commitment from Epic 5's retro actually worked.** This is the single clearest process win of Epic 6: naming an owner failed to close this gap across four straight epics (2-5); attaching a hard mechanism (land the PR or get a dated accept) at the Epic 5 retro closed it in one. The retro-escalation → hard-mechanism → resolution pipeline is now demonstrated as a repeatable process, not a one-off.

2. **Immutability testing needs to specifically probe reversal and identity/FK columns, not just content mutation.** The 6.4 trigger review found that a "we tested mutation rejection" suite can still miss a state-machine reversal (disarming the lock by flipping status back) and identity-column re-parenting (moving a row to a different logical owner while leaving content untouched). This is now a documented gap-class for any future immutability/lock trigger, not just a one-story finding.

3. **A golden fixture's negative assertions must not be allowed to be empty.** The `mustNotAppear: []` trap recurred (last seen conceptually in Epic 5's "no re-verification of resumed-run artifacts" family, but this is a distinct, testing-specific instance): an empty negative-assertion list looks like coverage but provides zero. This needs to become a lint-level or review-checklist rule, not just something a human reviewer happens to notice.

4. **Frozen-snapshot-by-copy is now proven a fourth time, at full quote-composite scale, and extended to the PDF layer.** Epic 3 (snapshot contract) → Epic 4 (golden fixture freeze) → Epic 5 (pricing-source row freeze) → Epic 6 (quote-version snapshot freeze AND PDF-source-of-truth, both proven via mutate-then-regenerate). This is the single most load-bearing piece of infrastructure the project has built, and Epic 6 is the first place a customer actually sees its output.

5. **Story-level scope notes materially undercounted 6.3's real complexity**, and the resulting "only one schema change" claim was falsified by an AC's own requirement (the lifecycle event). This is a narrower, milder version of the "trusted-recorded-state-over-reality" pattern from Epic 5's retro — not a resumed-run artifact this time, but a create-story-time scope estimate that didn't survive contact with implementation. Worth folding into create-story practice: a PDF/storage-touching story should assume it needs the full lifecycle-event surface, not just the state column.

6. **The owner/accounting/legal sign-off backlog did not grow this epic — it got a decision instead.** Epic 5's retro flagged this as "growing, not shrinking" and now-urgent because Epic 6's output is customer-visible. The 2026-07-03 owner decision (demo-data-only through MVP, real sign-off deferred until real-customer use is proposed) is a legitimate resolution of the *ambiguity*, even though the underlying sign-off session still hasn't happened. The risk (R-610) is now explicitly dated and has a named re-score trigger, which is a materially better state than an undated, silently-growing backlog.
